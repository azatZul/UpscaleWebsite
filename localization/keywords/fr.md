**`en ligne` beats `application` 179:1, and this is the defining fact of the locale.** Japan inverted Russia — `アプリ` 47% against `オンライン` 0.1% — and France inverts Japan just as hard. French searchers want a browser tool. Two SERP checks confirm it at the result level rather than the query level:

- **`améliorer qualité image`** (50 000/mo): iLoveIMG · Fotor · Pixelcut · Picsart · imgupscaler · Canva · enhancephoto.ai · aienhancer.ai · Adobe Express — **nine of nine are browser tools, no app in the top ten**
- **`image flou`** (5 000/mo): imgupscaler · Fotor · PicWish · beart · airbrush · imagy · evoto · Media.io — same picture, and eight of the ten are *unblur* rather than *add blur*, which settles the intent question below

**The `+iPhone` play that carries German and Japanese titles does not exist for French photo terms.** `améliorer photo iphone` is 50/mo, and `iphone` totals 550/mo across the whole photo corpus. German `<title>`s name iPhone because the platform qualifier is how we sidestep unwinnable head terms; in French there is nothing to sidestep *with*. The exception is video speed — see the second corpus.

**`IA` wins outright, and this is the opposite of Japanese.** `ia` 48 850 against `ai` 1 650 and `intelligence artificielle` 350 across 1 786 queries. Japan settled on Latin `AI` and a literal zero for 人工知能; France settles on the French initialism. **Never write `AI` in French copy, and never spell it out.**

**`image` beats `photo` on the head term 10:1.** `améliorer qualité image` **50 000** [25] against `améliorer qualité photo` 5 000 [44] — two exact synonyms, one order of magnitude apart. This is the German `Bild` : `Foto` lesson (40:1) repeating. **Prefer `image` on every SEO surface**; `photo` stays correct in body copy about a specific photograph.

**Blur: the App Store reading was wrong, and the correction is in our favour.** ASO showed `floutage` p52 and it looked like the Japanese `写真ぼかし` trap — demand for *adding* blur. The web says adding blur is 6 350/mo (0.8%) while the blurry-photo family is 62 950/mo, and it is the cheapest large cluster in French: `image flou` 5 000 **[0]** · `flou image` 5 000 [3] · `photo floue` 5 000 [5] · `flou photo` 5 000 [7]. The SERP check above confirms the intent is 80% ours. **Phrase it `image flou` / `photo floue`.** `déflouter` is the correct verb and only 500/mo at [42] — it belongs in the body, not the title.

**Restoration is not a French topic, and the scanning hypothesis was wrong too.** 5 050/mo across 29 queries with nothing above 500 — the same shape Japan showed (4 650/mo). ASO had suggested France scans rather than restores (`scanner photo` p18, Photomyne), but the web disqualifies that reading: `scanner des photos` [99], `scanner des négatifs` [100] and `numériser des négatifs` [100] are maximum-competition SERPs belonging to commercial developing labs. **Deprioritise `restore-old-photos` for `/fr/` and do not pivot it to `numériser`.** Where the old-photo intent actually surfaces is colorization — see below.

**`depixeliser une image` 5 000 [22]** is a French term with no English or German counterpart in our map, and no page targets it. It belongs in the 4K guide's body copy.

**The iPhone qualifier is alive in French — but only for video speed.** 32 queries carry `iphone` in this corpus, 4 700/mo, and they are the cheapest traffic measured anywhere in French: `ralentir video iphone` 500 **[5]** · `ralentir une video sur iphone` 500 **[6]** · `modifier vitesse video iphone` 500 [7] · `ralentir vitesse video iphone` 50 **[0]** · `iphone ralentir une video` 50 **[0]** · `faire un slow motion iphone` 50 [2]. In the 1 786-query photo corpus `iphone` was 550/mo total; here it is 8% of the cluster. This is the same shape as Japanese `iphone動画スロー再生` at competition 0, and it makes **the slow-motion guide the one French page where the German title formula applies unchanged.**

**The verb is `ralentir`, not `ralenti` and not `slow motion`.** Verb forms carry the cluster; `faire un slow motion iphone` is 50/mo. The bare English `slow motion` 5 000 [1] from the direct lookup is generic and largely not tool intent.

**`accélérer` is a trap inside the slow-motion cluster.** 5 400/mo for speeding video up, an operation UScale does not perform, and it is grammatically tangled with ours — `ralentir une video acceleree iphone` means *undo* a sped-up clip. The guide must say what it does and not reach for this traffic.

**Colorization is where the French old-photo intent actually lives.** 23 750/mo, head `coloriser une photo` / `colorisation photo` 5 000 [16], and **`coloriser une photo ancienne` 500 [17]** sits inside it. The restoration cluster is dead at 5 050/mo while colorization is nearly 5× that. **The short form is the head** — `coloriser une photo` 5 000 against `coloriser une photo noir et blanc` 500 — which is the exact opposite of Japanese, where the long form won 10:1.

**`négatif photo` is cheap, large and not ours.** 5 000/mo at competition [2] is the second-lowest-competition real term in French, and the SERP disqualifies it: Wikipedia *Film négatif* · NEGATIF+ (a developing lab) · «Inverser les couleurs d'une image» from Fotor, ImageMatrix and Pixelied · and *espace négatif*, which is composition theory. The query means "apply a negative filter", "get my film developed" or "what is a negative", never "I hold a negative and want a photo". The forms that are ours are the small ones: `negatif argentique` 500 [3] · `pellicule photo negatif` 50 [6] · `vieux négatifs photos` 50 [10]. **Same trap shape as Japanese `写真復元`: the big number belongs to a neighbouring meaning.**

**Rules for the French copy**, all evidence-backed above:

- **`IA`, never `AI`, never `intelligence artificielle`** — 48 850 : 1 650 : 350
- **`image` over `photo` on every SEO surface** — 10:1 on the head term
- **`en ligne` and `gratuit` are the two modifiers that exist** — 17.3% and 10.7%; `application` is 0.1% and cannot be leaned on
- **Do not put iPhone in French titles** — except the slow-motion guide, where it is the cheapest traffic in the locale
- **`ralentir`, not `ralenti`; never reach for `accélérer`**
- **Avoid `retouche`, `montage` and `modifier` entirely** — 54% of the market and none of it is ours
- **Write `image flou` / `photo floue` in titles, `déflouter` in the body**
- Accents in prose always; keyword strings follow the ASO-measured spelling
